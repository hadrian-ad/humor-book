Nutrients

Where do books come from? It's been common in academic circles to use a metaphor for waterways: books have "sources" that flow into the pool of the book.

There's something alchemical about the idea: that you could pick up a vial of one book, combine it with the vial of another, and the result is something new that came from the ingredients.

If you believe, as I do, that books come from people more than their ingredients, you might prefer other metaphors—like in some Islamic tradition, where the metaphor isn't waterways but nutrients. A book grows from the soil of a life.

I'd like to share some of the nutrients that went into this book, as you may well wonder what I found humorous or inspiring or important that creating the conditions that this book grew out of.

For this book, I did no literature review of humor or analogous terms, which was outside the scope and purpose of this project. My goal was to kindle something in the reader, not relate this book to other books.

Poetry

D.H. Lawrence, whose poetic work often focuses on the meaning of genuine lifeness.

Rumi, who grounds purpose in a kind of ecstatic humor—conveyed through particularly beautiful metaphors and inversions.

Thomas Hardy, whose poems often strike me as smiling in some way, and whose final volume of poetry (Winter in Various Moods and Meters) was kindly adapted for this work.

Teaching and Perspective

Drawing on the Right Side of the Brain, which created awareness of what effective book-based instruction actually looked like.

The Cartoon History of the Universe series, whose use of fitted humor to interpret history had indelible power. I have learned much from Gonick's bibliography, which had the simple improvement of a brief authorial reflection.

Spaces Speak, Are you Listening?, a book about attention to the context of sounds, which was an early example for me of teaching something everyone is aware of but rarely thinks about.

Rethinking Sitting, similar to above.

Radical Mycology

Wanderings on the Way (The Zhuangzi), anti-literacy and anti-moralism perspectives.

Tools of Conviviality, a book about the distinction of people using tools and people being tools of their tools.

The Phenomenon of Life an anti-mechanistic perspective on the world, whose style of presentation moved me.

Eros and Magic in the Renaissance, a scholarly book on untangling the jumbled but still coherent psychology of the Renaissance.
