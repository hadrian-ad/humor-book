Annotating humor tells in videos

(Video example for sections 8.1-8.2, "Humor tells express humor and intensity of humor")

We can annotate humor tells in videos, just like we can with pictures.

(15 seconds): https://youtu.be/bdVohQNmTAk?t=165

In this performance, something happens that causes the singer to feel humor. Her face brightens, she gains energy, her eyes activate, she smiles, her posture loosens and relaxes, she has a reduction of physical tension. She loses some situational awareness and focuses on the present. She wants to spread humor to the guitarist, and does this by social seeking and social tightening (by turning to look at the guitarist). Compared to when she simply sings the song without the same amount of humor, we see her humor as corresponding to a moment of increased agency.

The guitarist eventually notices or remembers what made the singer feel humor, and he smiles, brightens, loosens, and so on. He loses some situational awareness as he socially tightens with the singer by turning toward her.

Connecting humor tells and humor family words

(Video examples for sections 8.5-8.6, "humor tells express humor family words")

We can see humor tells expressing humor family words in videos.

Sumo wrestling.
(10 seconds): https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k2itSyAO3Qs&t=47s

As the victorious wrestler stands, he smiles, brightens, has expansive posture, loosens, has high energy (relative to the other wrestler), has increased agency (relative to the other wrestler), has raised spirits, socially tightens (by moving closer to the other wrestler), and so on.

Since humor tells and humor family words are the same thing ("humor"), we can describe the victorious sumo as feeling accomplishment, achievement, comfort, joy, realization, release, relief, safety, success, and so on. (Note that the crowd is also showing humor tells—and more intensely than the wrestler in this clip.)

Interview
(45 seconds): https://youtu.be/HamYmjllE6A?t=223

The woman's humor tells show accomplishment, achievement, delight, and success as she finishes the song to applause. When asked a question, her humor tells show comfort, safety, and welcome. When she's surprised by something the man says and learn about it, her humor tells show insight and realization. Throughout the conversation, her humor tells show appreciation, connection, belonging, joy, and so on.

While she laughs several times and smiles throughout, we should look beyond those too for other humor tells: like how she has energy, flushes, moves informally with informal posture (swaying), touches self (hands clasped), social seeking and social tightening, focused on present, raised spirits, and so on.

The man shows far fewer humor tells, and so we wouldn't characterize their emotional state as identical.

Wartime occupation

When the camera cuts to the audience, we see them staggered (informal posture, informal movement, loosening, relaxed posture, non-normative behavior), with red faces (flushing) and complete loss of situational awareness. They at least briefly feel optimism, raised spirits, reduction of stress, and feel social integration. They touch each other.

These humor tells express humor family words: the audience feels acceptance, appreciation, connection, delight, forgiveness, reconciliation, release, revelation, relief, and so on.

Because the humor tells are so intense, we'd say these humor family words were also correspondingly intense (intense appreciation, intense release, and so on).

Because the humor was such a long duration, we'd say that the mistake went against durable expectations.

Note that just because we can describe the soldiers' humor as "intense reconciliation (with the situation)," that doesn't tell us how long this feeling will persist; what actions will be taken because of it; and so on. ("Oh! They all must have had a great time with their under-armored humvees.")

Act of friendship:

The friend who gets the scarf feels realization (as she understands what happens), comfort, generosity, belonging, appreciation, release, safety, welcome, and so on—and so smiles, social seeks, social tightens, brightens, relaxes, and so on.

The friend who applies the scarf at first doesn't seem to feel humor, but we'll notice she increasingly smiles throughout the clip. We can see her humor more clearly when we look for humor tells other than just smiling and laughing: she touches others, is making an effort to spread humor, is focusing on present, shows increased agency (relative to right before), socially tightens, tries to increase social integration, and so on.

Because of these humor wells, we can describe both of their emotional states with similar humor family words—even if one of them feels this more intensely than the other.
